# Business Customer Risk Assessment Policy

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Policy** | Business customer risk assessment (Know Your Business) |
| **Version** | 1.0, November 2026 |
| **Owner** | Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) |
| **Applies to** | Onboarding and periodic review of business customers in the United Kingdom and India |
| **Review** | At least annually, and whenever the law, regulatory guidance or the firm's risk assessment changes |

> **Important notice.** This is a model policy published with VeriKYB, an open-source Know Your
> Business tool. It has not been adopted by any regulated firm, and it is not legal or compliance
> advice. It shows how a firm can write risk rules that software applies and cites. A firm that
> adopts it must adapt it to its own business-wide risk assessment, and have it approved by its
> MLRO and senior management.

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how the firm identifies, assesses and records the money laundering,
terrorist financing and sanctions risk of its business customers. It turns legal obligations into
specific rules, so that every customer is assessed in the same way, and so that every risk
indicator raised during an assessment can be traced to the rule that requires it.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every business customer (companies, limited liability partnerships and
other legal persons) when a relationship is established and at each periodic review. It covers
beneficial ownership, ownership structure, sanctions, politically exposed persons, adverse media,
filing and financial health, regulatory status, documents, jurisdictions, and the setting and
approval of the risk tier.

## 3. Regulatory basis

The rules in this policy implement, in particular:

- **United Kingdom:** the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information
  on the Payer) Regulations 2017, including regulation 5 (beneficial owners of bodies corporate),
  regulation 28 (customer due diligence), regulation 33 (enhanced due diligence) and regulation 35
  (politically exposed persons); the financial sanctions regime administered by the Office of
  Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI).
- **India:** the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002, the Prevention of Money-laundering
  (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005, and the Reserve Bank of India Master Direction on Know Your
  Customer, 2016.
- **International standards:** the FATF Recommendations, in particular Recommendation 10 (customer
  due diligence), Recommendation 12 (politically exposed persons), Recommendation 19 (higher-risk
  countries) and Recommendation 24 (transparency and beneficial ownership of legal persons).

Where local law sets a stricter requirement than this policy, local law prevails.

## 4. How to read this policy

Each rule has a permanent identifier, such as POL-1.2. Every risk indicator raised during an
assessment cites the rule that requires it, and every decision can be traced back to that rule.
A **high-risk indicator** makes the customer's risk tier high; a **medium-risk indicator** makes it
at least medium (section POL-10). "Must" marks a requirement; no exception applies without the
approval described in section POL-10.

## 5. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Beneficial owner | A natural person who ultimately owns or controls the customer, directly or through other entities, above the threshold in POL-1.1 |
| Senior managing official | The natural person who exercises executive control over the customer, recorded when no beneficial owner can be identified |
| Politically exposed person (PEP) | A person entrusted with a prominent public function, as defined in the applicable law |
| Possible match | A name on a sanctions or PEP list that resembles a customer, owner or director, before it is adjudicated |
| Confirmed match | A possible match that adjudication shows to be the same person or entity |
| Adverse media | Reports in credible public sources alleging wrongdoing |
| Reviewer | The person who decides whether to approve a customer; a second reviewer is a different person |
| Risk tier | The customer's overall risk: low, medium or high, set as described in POL-10 |

## POL-1 Beneficial ownership

POL-1.1 The firm must identify every natural person who ultimately owns or controls the customer
above the threshold for its jurisdiction: more than 25% of the shares or voting rights in the
United Kingdom, and more than 10% in India. Indirect ownership counts: the holdings are multiplied
along each chain of intermediate entities, and the results are added together across chains.

POL-1.2 Where a registry discloses a holding as a band rather than an exact figure, the whole band
must be taken into account. Where the band straddles the threshold, the person must be treated as
a possible beneficial owner, and the exact holding must be obtained before approval.

POL-1.3 Where no beneficial owner can be identified, the senior managing official must be recorded,
together with the reasons why no beneficial owner could be identified, and the case must be
escalated.

## POL-2 Ownership structure

POL-2.1 Circular ownership, in which an entity owns itself through a chain of other entities,
obscures who controls the customer. It is a high-risk indicator and must be escalated.

POL-2.2 Where ownership runs through more than three layers of entities, the analyst must document
each layer with supporting evidence.

## POL-3 Sanctions

POL-3.1 A confirmed sanctions match on the customer, a beneficial owner or a director requires the
relationship to be declined or ended, and the match to be reported to the MLRO, who decides on any
report to the competent authority.

POL-3.2 A director or owner of the customer who also directs or owns a sanctioned entity is a
high-risk indicator, even where that person is not listed.

POL-3.3 A possible match must not be cleared until it has been adjudicated against the date of
birth, nationality and other available identifiers, and the reason for clearing it must be
recorded.

## POL-4 Politically exposed persons

POL-4.1 A politically exposed person among the customer's owners, directors or controllers requires
enhanced due diligence, including establishing the source of wealth and the source of funds, and
approval of the relationship by a second reviewer.

## POL-5 Adverse media

POL-5.1 Credible adverse media alleging financial crime, sanctions evasion or fraud involving the
customer or its key individuals is a high-risk indicator. Reports that have not yet been verified
are a medium-risk indicator until they have been assessed.

## POL-6 Filing and financial health

POL-6.1 Overdue annual accounts or an overdue confirmation statement is a medium-risk indicator.

POL-6.2 Insolvency proceedings, whether current or past, are a high-risk indicator.

POL-6.3 Outstanding charges must be recorded, but are not a risk indicator on their own.

## POL-7 Regulatory status

POL-7.1 A customer carrying on regulated activities must hold the corresponding permissions from
the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). A customer that is not authorised, or whose authorisation
has been cancelled, is a high-risk indicator.

POL-7.2 Where the customer's FCA permissions have changed since the last review, the analyst must
confirm that its expected activity remains within the permissions it holds. Any disciplinary
history is a high-risk indicator.

## POL-8 Documents

POL-8.1 A company incorporated in India must provide its Certificate of Incorporation, its Permanent
Account Number (PAN) card, its Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration certificate and a current
shareholding extract. Where any of these is missing, further information must be requested.

POL-8.2 A document that has passed its validity date must be replaced before approval.

POL-8.3 Where an address on a document differs from the registry record, the customer must explain
the difference before approval.

## POL-9 Jurisdictions

POL-9.1 A connection to a jurisdiction on the FATF list of high-risk jurisdictions subject to a call
for action is a high-risk indicator. A connection to a jurisdiction under increased monitoring by
the FATF is a medium-risk indicator.

## POL-10 Risk tier and approval

POL-10.1 The risk tier is set by the rules in this policy: any high-risk indicator makes the tier
high; otherwise, any medium-risk indicator makes it medium; otherwise, it is low.

POL-10.2 A reviewer may override the suggested risk tier only with a recorded reason. Approval of a
high-risk customer requires a second, independent reviewer.

## 6. Record keeping

The evidence gathered for each assessment, the risk indicators raised, the rules they cite, and
every decision and override with its reason must be kept for at least five years after the
business relationship ends, or longer where local law requires it.

## 7. Governance

The MLRO owns this policy and reviews it at least once a year, and whenever the law, regulatory
guidance or the firm's business-wide risk assessment changes. Changes must be approved by senior
management. Rule identifiers are permanent: a withdrawn rule's identifier is never reused, so
that past decisions keep pointing at the rule they applied.
